## Burgers characteristic obstruction

`candidate.lean` defines the scalar state-only flux `burgersFlux q = q²/2`, proves its derivative is `q`, and proves it cannot be a scalar linear flux `q ↦ c*q`. `IsClassicalBurgersOn u Du T` requires an independently supplied real spacetime field `u`, a continuous actual Fréchet derivative `Du`, and the Burgers PDE on the strip `0 ≤ t ≤ T`. The derivative hypotheses bind `Du` to `u` through `HasFDerivAt`; they contain no assumed characteristic identity or contradiction certificate. The conservation-form equivalence proves that this PDE is exactly `∂ₜu + ∂ₓ(burgersFlux ∘ u) = 0` under those differentiability hypotheses.

For a characteristic starting at `ξ` with the actual initial value `v`, the proof sets `e(t)=u(ξ+v*t,t)-v`. The Fréchet chain rule and PDE give `e' = -uₓ(ξ+v*t,t)*e`. Continuity on the compact time interval supplies a finite coefficient bound. Mathlib's Grönwall zero-uniqueness lemma then proves `e=0`; the transport conclusion is proved rather than assumed. Consequently two characteristics carrying distinct values cannot collide while a classical solution exists.

The concrete smooth initial data are `u₀(x)=-sin x`. A separate theorem proves `ContDiff ℝ ⊤ u₀` and `|u₀(x)|≤1` for every real `x`. At `ξ=-π/2` the value is `+1`; at `ξ=+π/2` it is `-1`. Both characteristics reach `x=0` at time `π/2`. The collision theorem therefore excludes a classical solution on the stated strip with those initial data.

This result gives a finite upper bound for possible classical continuation. It does not assert that `π/2` is the first singular time. It does not construct a post-breakdown weak solution, prove an entropy condition, or prove that a discontinuity actually exists. In particular, it cannot close `LEV-CH01-NONLINEAR-SHOCK-FORMATION` by itself. Derivative and derivative-continuity assumptions are made on ambient `ℝ×ℝ`, with the PDE only on the finite strip; a formulation using one-sided regularity only at strip endpoints would require a separate strengthening of the API. The initial data are bounded and smooth but are not compactly supported or integrable on the whole real line.
